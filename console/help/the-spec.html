The spec is the contract a build is held to: everything the feature must do, and where it stops. You read it, then approve it or send it back. Approving starts the build.

How it's written

The spec writer turns your intent and answers into a spec. The spec checker reads the draft the way the blind test writer will, and objects wherever a criterion can't be tested or misses what you said. The feature shows writing the spec while they work, then awaiting freeze when it's yours to read.

Reading it

  • In practice: what it's like today, and once this ships.
  • What it touches: the data, interfaces and screens it changes, checked later against what the workers actually build.
  • What it must do: the acceptance criteria, the complete promise. Each is tagged with the lowest test level it can be checked at; one Fabrika won't test in this project is tagged you check. See Test levels.
  • Where it stops: what it's deliberately not doing, and what it must respect.
  • What could be wrong: what it assumes, what nobody decided, and anything that cannot be undone, such as a dropped column.
  • How this was decided: your answers and corrections.

Your ruling

Send it back if it has something wrong: say what, and the interrogator asks again from your correction and the spec is rewritten. Nothing has been built yet.

Approve & build starts the build. There's no further input until it produces a packet, unless the architect and the plan checker disagree about how to cut the work (see Plan review). If a subscription the run needs is near the end of its usage window, you're offered Freeze & start at the reset time, with Start anyway beside it. See Builds that wait for a plan window.
