At review you can open the feature's app in your browser and look at it. Fabrika starts it from the feature's branch, the same way it starts it for the checks, and only this machine can reach it.

Why it's there

Review asks you to rule on things: criteria Fabrika couldn't test, and an argument that a page shows what the spec asked for. Looking at the page is the quickest way to judge both. Without this you'd check out the branch, install it, and find free ports next to your own dev servers.

What happens when you press it

  • Fabrika runs the environment's setup in the feature's worktree, then starts its services, runs its preparation steps and creates the test database, exactly as it does before the checks.
  • It asks the page for an answer, and gives you the link once it gets one. This takes about as long as setup takes; the button's tooltip says about how long, from the last run.
  • Your ruling on a criterion you check by hand records that the app was open, and the commit it was running.

What it can and can't reach

The app stays on the same sealed network the checks run on. It can't reach the internet or anything else on this machine, so a call to a payment provider or a map service fails there. That says nothing about the feature. Its data is whatever the environment's preparation seeds.

Fabrika forwards each service's port to 127.0.0.1 under the same number, so the addresses the app was given work in your browser. Nothing is reachable from other machines.

When it closes

When you press Stop, when you rule on the feature or send it for more work, and after an hour with the review page closed. Restarting Fabrika closes it too.

When there's no button

Fabrika needs to know which service a person opens. The survey says so in the environment, and the project reading checks that it opens on the main branch. If the survey couldn't tell, survey again, or set it on the environment. A library or a command-line tool has nothing to open, and its environment says so.
