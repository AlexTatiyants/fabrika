Objections are everything the review raised about the change, security included. Blind spots are the places where the run's own figures are worth less than they look.

Who objects

The breaker attacks the finished code and proves each break with a test that fails. The reviewer reads the whole change and makes the strongest case against shipping it, and any review agent you add on the crew page reads the same bundle; none of them can see what the others said. The arbiter then decides what happens to each finding: real or not, fixable, or yours to rule on.

Objections

At the top is the strongest case against shipping this, in its proponent's words. Then every finding, in bands by what the repair loop did about it: The loop tried to fix these and could not, The loop never tried these, An agent argued these away, The loop fixed these. Each row carries its severity and its category, so a security finding is found by its category, such as authz or exposure. Below the bands, what each agent examined and found sound.

Nothing is dropped for having been fixed: a packet that got shorter as the loop worked would look better and be worth less. A finding that restates another is folded under it.

Blind spots

Every figure in a packet is computed as though everything meant to run did run. Where that isn't true, the thing that failed is the one thing that can't report it: an agent that never returned files no finding, and a suite that died early names no failures. Blind spots are computed, not reported. Each one names the figure it makes look better, and is marked discount or, where nothing was measured at all, nothing measured. The figures under Stats carry a † into the blind spot that discounts them.

Both tabs end with a box for something in your own words. It's filed as a flag, tagged repair unless you choose otherwise, and joins the card at the foot of Your calls. See Your calls.
