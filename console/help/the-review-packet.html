When a build finishes you get a review packet: the code on its own branch, and an argument that it does what the spec asked. You audit the argument and rule on what it leaves you, rather than reading every line.

The verdict

Across the top is the verdict the rapporteur recommends, and a one-sentence headline saying why: Ships, Ships, with rulings, Do not ship or Reject. It's advice; the ruling is yours. When some criteria were left for you to check by hand, the verdict is never better than Ships, with rulings, and the headline says how many are left.

  • Stats opens the figures behind the verdict: blockers open, checks passing, criteria verified, probes failing, findings raised, the minutes of your attention it budgets, what the repair loop cost, and which review agents read it. A figure marked † is worth less than it looks; the dagger opens the blind spot that discounts it.
  • Open the app starts the feature's app from its branch, so you can look at it. See Opening the app at review.

The tabs

  • Your calls: the decisions this run left for you. Start here. See Your calls.
  • Work map: how each criterion connects to the files that changed, and the way into either. See The work map, a criterion, a file.
  • Evidence and Dependencies: what the run produced that you can check yourself, and the packages the change brought in. See Evidence and dependencies.
  • Objections and Blind spots: everything the review raised, and where the figures are worth less than they look. See Objections and blind spots.
  • Repairs: what the repair loop changed in each round. See Repairs and the repair loop.

A red dot on a tab means something in it is still standing; an amber dot means evidence is missing there. The count on Your calls is the only one that goes down. F switches to reading mode.

Ruling

When you're done, Rule on this packet, in the bar, takes you to the ruling. Verify again and Build again sit beside it. See Ruling on the packet and Verify again, build again, resume, discard.
