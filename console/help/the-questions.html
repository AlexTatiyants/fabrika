Before a spec is written, Fabrika asks you what the code can't tell it. You get one round of questions: answer them, then freeze.

First, check its reading

At the top is What it thinks you asked for: the interrogator's restatement of your intent. If it's wrong, press That's not what I meant and say what it is. That replaces its reading entirely, and every question is asked again from your words (Ask again from this). If what the scout saw was wrong or incomplete, Read the codebase again reads the repository from scratch, which costs more calls.

Answering

Each question is marked blocking, significant or minor. Pick one of its options, or open why this matters · write your own to read the reasoning and type your own answer (Use this), or take the proposed default (Use the default). An answered question stays in its row, marked settled; change reopens it.

Freezing

Freeze the spec, in the bar, closes the questions. It stays disabled until every blocking question has an answer: a wrong guess on one of those throws work away rather than adjusting it. Anything else you leave unanswered is carried into the spec as accepted risk, and named there. The spec writer then writes the spec from your answers. See The spec, and approving it.

If the interrogator raised nothing, read its restatement closely. If it captures what you meant, your intent was tight enough to build from; if it dropped something, correct it rather than freezing.

Later, the questions step shows what was asked and answered, read-only.
