Most builds never stop here. Plan review happens only when the plan checker disputes how the architect cut the work, or code measures a problem with the cut. You rule, and your ruling starts the build.

Why there's a cut

The architect cuts the approved spec into units that workers build side by side. Before any worker starts, the plan checker takes each builder's seat and objects wherever one of them would have to guess. The architect answers. Whatever they still disagree about comes to you, and the feature shows awaiting plan review.

What you see

  • Where they disagree: each objection, with the architect's answer beside the plan checker's.
  • What code measured: facts about the cut, not opinions. You can still keep the cut; the problem goes into the packet as a risk you accepted.
  • The cut itself, folded: its units, the criteria each one covers, and the seams between them.

Your ruling

  • Agree with architect: build the cut as it stands. What the checker objected to goes into the packet as a risk you accepted.
  • Agree with checker, or Re-cut to fix it when only code objected: the architect re-cuts once. If code still measures a problem, you're asked again.
  • Propose alternative: write how you would cut it, and the architect re-cuts that way.

No worker runs until you rule. After that the build carries on without you. See Watching the build.
