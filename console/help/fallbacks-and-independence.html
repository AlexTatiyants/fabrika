Some agents exist to disagree with another, and two models from the same vendor agreeing is one blind spot rendered twice. Fabrika checks that each such pair stays on different model families, today and on the day a route runs out.

The pairs

  • the oracle, the reviewer and the breaker must differ from the worker;
  • the arbiter from the reviewer;
  • the spec_checker from the spec_writer;
  • the plan_checker from the architect.

A family is the route, when an agent names one: two agents on one subscription talk to the same vendor whatever model each picked. Otherwise it's the vendor at the front of the model id. It's coarse on purpose, there to catch every agent ending up on one model.

A pair that shares a family is reported, never refused: running everything on one family for a while is a legitimate thing to do. What mustn't happen is that it becomes true without anyone saying so. The crew page warns, and every packet built that way carries a finding saying its reviewers share a family with the work they challenge.

Fallback lanes

When an agent's route refuses a call, at its usage limit or out of credit, the agent can move to a fallback instead of stopping the run: one hop, to another route and model. Fallbacks are named lanes in factory.yaml, and having two is the point. The agents that build land in one and the agents that check land in the other, so the pairs above stay apart on the worst day as well as a good one. An agent with no fallback stops the run when its route says no.

The crew page's Fallback lanes section shows each lane, its route and model, and the agents on it, and says whether any pair would stop disagreeing once a fallback is taken. A fallback taken during a run is noted in that run's packet.

Not the same thing: the fallbacks switch in an agent's sheet is whether a provider that serves a model from several hosts may move the call to another host.
