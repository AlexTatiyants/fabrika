A feature's stage is what you can do with it next, and the console opens the screen that stage needs. These are the words on the rail and in a project's feature list.

The stages

  • intake: the scout and the interrogator are reading the repository.
  • awaiting answers: the questions are waiting on you.
  • writing the spec: the spec writer and the spec checker are at work.
  • awaiting freeze: the spec is written, and waits for you to approve it or send it back.
  • awaiting plan review: the architect and the plan checker disagree about the cut, and you rule.
  • waiting for the plan: approved, and held until a subscription's usage window resets.
  • building: the build and its review are running. Nothing is needed from you.
  • awaiting verdict: a packet is ready for your ruling.
  • accepted or rejected: you've ruled. Everything stays on the record.
  • failed: a station stopped the line. It can be resumed.

A stage that waits on you is drawn in the accent colour, and the rail's Needs you filter shows only those. A packet waiting on you also says which way it leans: ready, rulings, do not ship or reject.

The steps

Under a feature's name is a row of steps: intent · scout · questions · spec · build · review, and then runs. The stage decides where you land, not what you may look at: any step whose work exists can be opened. A step the feature has moved past is read as it was, and nothing on it can be changed. The step that owes you something says what, such as questions · you.

runs is every run of the feature, the first build, each verify again and each repair round, as a timeline and a call tree. See Watching the build.
